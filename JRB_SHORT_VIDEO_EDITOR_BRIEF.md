## 4. The whole idea in very simple language

Imagine that the video is telling a child a story in ten small steps:

1. Tell us what the story is about.
2. Let the Minister say something important.
3. Tell us the name of the programme.
4. Introduce the Minister properly.
5. Let him explain one benefit of the reform.
6. Briefly show that this was a real conversation between two people.
7. Show the question being discussed.
8. Let the Minister give the main answer.
9. Remind us of the programme.
10. Finish with the JRB logo.

The audience should never need to guess what they are looking at. At every moment, they should understand either:

- who is speaking;
- what question is being answered;
- what the important idea is; or
- who produced the programme.

This follows the most useful principle from the selected Cleo Abram/Jensen Huang YouTube reference: begin with an important idea, explain only one idea at a time, and keep changing the supporting visual while the speaker’s voice continues.

It must remain Mr Effiong’s exact structure: the Opening Montage first, followed by Slides 1–9. The YouTube reference should influence the clarity, pacing and visual storytelling—not change his order or wording.

### What this means in simple language

The screen begins quietly. The title enters from above, one group of words at a time. The sound should feel serious and important, like the beginning of a respected documentary—not like a game, comedy video or nightclub advertisement.

### Precise visual treatment

1. Begin with **six frames of near-black**, not pure black.

   - `One Year`

“Classical” should mean restrained orchestral music—not a dramatic trailer boom. Avoid cymbal crashes, thunder, digital glitches and repeated whooshes.
